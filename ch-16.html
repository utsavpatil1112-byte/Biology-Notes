1 16.1 Nitrogenous Wastes & Modes of Excretion

NCERT Page 205-206

Metabolic Accumulation & Excretion Concept

Animals accumulate ammonia, urea, uric acid, carbon dioxide, water and ions like Na+, K+, Cl-, phosphate, sulphate, etc., either by metabolic activities or by excess ingestion. These nitrogenous products are derived mainly from protein and nucleic acid catabolism and must be eliminated from the body totally or partially.

1. Ammonotelism

Max Toxicity
  • • Excretory Product: Ammonia (NH₃)
  • • Toxicity: Extremely toxic; requires large amounts of water for elimination.
  • • Excretion Mode: Readily soluble; excreted by diffusion across body surface or gill surfaces as ammonium ions (NH₄+).
  • • Kidney Role: Kidneys play no significant role in its removal.
Textbook Examples: Many bony fishes, aquatic amphibians, and aquatic insects.

2. Ureotelism

Moderate Toxicity
  • • Excretory Product: Urea
  • • Adaptation: Terrestrial adaptation for water conservation.
  • • Formation Site: Ammonia produced by metabolism is converted into urea in the LIVER and released into blood.
  • • Matrix Retention: Some urea may be retained in kidney matrix to maintain desired osmolarity.
Textbook Examples: Mammals, many terrestrial amphibians, and marine fishes.

3. Uricotelism

Least Toxicity
  • • Excretory Product: Uric acid
  • • Physical State: Excreted as pellet or paste with minimum loss of water.
  • • Toxicity: Least toxic among all three nitrogenous wastes.
  • • Water Economy: Ideal for maximum conservation of body water.
Textbook Examples: Reptiles, birds, land snails, and insects.

2 Diversity of Excretory Structures Across the Animal Kingdom

Simple Tubular Structures (Invertebrates)

  • 1. Protonephridia / Flame Cells:

    Excretory structures in Platyhelminthes (Flatworms e.g., Planaria), rotifers, some annelids, and the cephalochordate Amphioxus. Primarily concerned with ionic and fluid volume regulation (osmoregulation).

  • 2. Nephridia:

    Tubular excretory structures of earthworms and other annelids. Help to remove nitrogenous wastes and maintain fluid/ionic balance.

Complex Tubular Organs (Insects, Crustaceans & Vertebrates)

  • 3. Malpighian Tubules:

    Excretory structures of most insects including cockroaches. Help in removal of nitrogenous wastes and osmoregulation.

  • 4. Antennal Glands / Green Glands:

    Perform excretory function in crustaceans like prawns.

  • 5. Kidneys:

    Complex tubular organs present in Vertebrates for filtration, reabsorption, secretion, and osmoregulation.

    4 Structure of the Nephron (Functional Unit of Kidney)

    NCERT Page 207-208
    Each human kidney contains nearly 1 million (10 lakh) complex tubular structures called nephrons, which are the functional units of the kidney. Each nephron consists of two main parts: (1) Glomerulus and (2) Renal Tubule.

    1. Glomerulus & Malpighian Body

    Filtration Unit
    • • Glomerulus: A tuft of capillary network formed by the afferent arteriole (a fine branch of renal artery). Blood from glomerulus is carried away by an efferent arteriole.
    • • Bowman's Capsule: Double-walled cup-like structure enclosing glomerulus.
    • • Malpighian Body (Renal Corpuscle): Glomerulus + Bowman's Capsule.
    • • Podocytes: Specialized epithelial cells of Bowman's capsule inner layer arranged intricately leaving minute spaces called filtration slits or slit pores.

    2. Renal Tubule Segments

    Tubular System
    • • Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT): Highly coiled network lined by simple cuboidal brush border epithelium (increases surface area).
    • • Henle's Loop: Hairpin-shaped tubule with descending limb (thin, permeable to water) and ascending limb (impermeable to water, transports NaCl).
    • • Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT): Highly coiled segment located in cortex opening into collecting duct.
    • • Collecting Duct: Long straight duct running from cortex to inner medulla; DCTs of many nephrons converge into it. Opens into renal pelvis through calyces.

    • Vasa Recta: A minute vessel of peritubular network running parallel to Henle's loop forming a 'U' shaped vessel. Absent/reduced in cortical nephrons; prominent in juxtamedullary nephrons.

    5 16.2 Urine Formation — Three Main Steps

    NCERT Page 208-209

    1. Glomerular Ultrafiltration

    Step 1
    • • Blood Volume Filtered: 1100–1200 mL of blood per minute (~1/5th of cardiac output pumped by each ventricle per min).
    • • Filtration Barrier (3 Layers):
      1. Endothelium of glomerular blood vessels
      2. Epithelium of Bowman's capsule (with Podocytes & Slit Pores)
      3. Basement membrane between the two layers
    • • Ultrafiltration Nature: Blood is filtered so finely that almost all constituents of plasma except proteins pass into lumen of Bowman's capsule.

    2. Tubular Reabsorption

    Step 2
    • • Massive Reabsorption: Out of 180 Litres filtrate/day, only 1.5 L urine is released. ~99% of filtrate is reabsorbed by renal tubules.
    • • Active Transport: Glucose, amino acids, Na+, etc. are reabsorbed actively against gradient.
    • • Passive Transport: Nitrogenous wastes are absorbed passively. Water reabsorption also occurs passively in initial segments.

    3. Tubular Secretion

    Step 3
    • • Secreted Substances: Tubular cells secrete H+, K+, and Ammonia (NH₃) into the filtrate.
    • • Crucial Function: Helps maintain ionic balance and acid-base (pH) balance of body fluids.

    📐 Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) Quantitative Values

    NCERT Standard
    GFR Per Minute 125 mL / minute
    GFR Per Day 180 Litres / day
    Actual Urine Output 1.5 Litres / day (~0.8%)

    Autoregulation by JGA: Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA) is a specialized region formed by cellular modifications in DCT and afferent arteriole at contact location. Fall in GFR → activates JG cells → releases Renin → stimulates glomerular blood flow → brings GFR back to normal.

    6 16.3 Functions of Different Nephron Tubules

    NCERT Page 209-210

    1. Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)

    70–80% Reabsorption
    • • Epithelium: Lined by simple cuboidal brush border epithelium which increases surface area for reabsorption.
    • • Reabsorption: Reabsorbs nearly all essential nutrients, and 70–80% of electrolytes and water.
    • • Secretion & pH Homeostasis: Maintains pH and ionic balance of body fluids by selective secretion of H+, K+, and NH₃ into filtrate, and reabsorption of HCO₃-.

    2. Loop of Henle

    Medullary Gradient
    • • General: Reabsorption is minimum in ascending limb. Plays crucial role in maintaining high osmolarity of medullary interstitial fluid.
    • • Descending Limb: Permeable to water, almost impermeable to electrolytes. Concentrates filtrate as it moves down.
    • • Ascending Limb: Impermeable to water, allows transport of electrolytes (NaCl) actively or passively. Concentrated filtrate gets diluted moving upward.

    3. Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)

    Conditional Reabsorption
    • • Reabsorption: Conditional reabsorption of Na+ and water takes place here (regulated by Aldosterone & ADH). Reabsorbs HCO₃-.
    • • Secretion: Selective secretion of H+, K+, and NH₃ to maintain blood pH and sodium-potassium balance.

    4. Collecting Duct (CD)

    Final Water Concentration
    • • Water Absorption: Extends from cortex to inner medulla. Large amounts of water reabsorbed to produce concentrated urine.
    • • Urea Diffusion: Allows passage of small amounts of urea into medullary interstitium to maintain high osmolarity.
    • • pH Balance: Secretes H+ and K+ ions for ionic balance.

    7 16.4 Mechanism of Concentration of the Filtrate (Counter-Current Mechanism)

    NCERT Page 210-212

    Core Principle of Counter-Current System

    Mammals have the ability to produce urine 4 times more concentrated than the initial filtrate (up to 1200 mOsmol L-1 compared to 300 mOsmol L-1 in cortex). This hyperosmolar gradient is established and maintained by two counter-current systems operating in close proximity: (1) Loop of Henle and (2) Vasa Recta.

    1. Counter-Current in Loop of Henle

    Filtrate flows in opposite directions in the two limbs of Henle's loop:

    • • Downward in descending limb (water moves out → filtrate concentrated)
    • • Upward in ascending limb (NaCl pumped out → filtrate diluted)

    2. Counter-Current in Vasa Recta

    Blood flows in opposite directions in the two limbs of Vasa Recta:

    • • Downward in descending limb (takes up NaCl from interstitium)
    • • Upward in ascending limb (returns NaCl back to interstitium)

    Osmolarity Gradient in Medullary Interstitium

    300 ➔ 1200 mOsmol L⁻¹
    Role of NaCl:

    NaCl transported by ascending limb of Henle's loop is exchanged with descending limb of Vasa Recta. NaCl is returned to interstitium by ascending portion of Vasa Recta.

    Role of Urea:

    Small amounts of urea enter thin segment of ascending limb of Henle's loop, which is transported back to medullary interstitium by collecting tubule to complete the loop.

    8 16.5 Regulation of Kidney Function (Hormonal Control)

    NCERT Page 212

    Kidney function is efficiently monitored and regulated by hormonal feedback mechanisms involving Hypothalamus, JGA, and to a certain extent, the Heart.

    1. Hypothalamus & ADH Axis

    Osmoreceptor Regulation
    1. Fluid loss / dehydration activates Osmoreceptors in body.
    2. Stimulates Hypothalamus to release Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) / Vasopressin from neurohypophysis.
    3. ADH facilitates water reabsorption from DCT and Collecting Duct → prevents Diuresis.
    4. Constricts blood vessels → increases BP → increases Glomerular Blood Flow & GFR.
    5. Increase in fluid volume switches off osmoreceptors (Negative Feedback).
    ⭐ Key Outcome: Conserves body water & elevates GFR.

    2. Renin-Angiotensin Mechanism (RAAS)

    JGA Endocrine Axis
    1. Fall in Glomerular Blood Flow / BP / GFR activates JG cells.
    2. JG cells release enzyme Renin into blood.
    3. Renin converts Angiotensinogen ➔ Angiotensin I ➔ Angiotensin II.
    4. Angiotensin II (powerful vasoconstrictor) increases BP and GFR.
    5. Angiotensin II activates Adrenal Cortex to release Aldosterone.
    6. Aldosterone causes reabsorption of Na+ and H₂O from distal tubules.
    ⭐ Key Outcome: Restores Blood Pressure & GFR back to normal.

    3. Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)

    Heart Feedback Check
    1. Increase in blood flow to atria of heart stimulates atrial wall.
    2. Atria release Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF).
    3. ANF causes Vasodilation (dilation of blood vessels).
    4. Decreases blood pressure.
    5. ANF mechanism acts as a check / antagonist to the Renin-Angiotensin mechanism.
    ⭐ Key Outcome: Lowers BP; counteracts RAAS overactivation.
    RAAS Cascade Flow Formula:
    Fall in GFR/BP ➔ JG Cells release Renin ➔ Angiotensinogen ➔ Angiotensin I ➔ Angiotensin II ➔ Aldosterone (Adrenal) ➔ Na⁺ & H₂O Reabsorption

    9 16.6 Micturition & Clinical Diagnostics of Urine

    NCERT Page 212-213

    The Micturition Reflex Pathway

    Urine formed by nephrons is stored in urinary bladder until voluntary CNS signal:

    1. Filling of urinary bladder with urine causes stretching of bladder walls.
    2. Stretch receptors on bladder walls send sensory signals to Central Nervous System (CNS).
    3. CNS passes motor messages to initiate simultaneous:
      • Contraction of smooth muscles of bladder
      • Relaxation of urethral sphincter
    4. Leads to release of urine (Micturition via Micturition Reflex).

    Normal Physical & Chemical Parameters

    Daily Volume 1.0 – 1.5 Litres / day
    pH Value Slightly Acidic (pH ~ 6.0)
    Color & Fluid Light yellow watery fluid
    Urea Excreted / Day 25 – 30 grams / day
    🩺 Clinical Diagnostic Indicators in Urine:

    • Glycosuria: Presence of glucose in urine.

    • Ketonuria: Presence of ketone bodies in urine.

    → Both Glycosuria and Ketonuria are indicative of Diabetes Mellitus.

    10 16.7 Role of Other Organs in Excretion

    NCERT Page 213

    Other than kidneys, lungs, liver, and skin also assist significantly in the elimination of metabolic excretory wastes.

    1. Lungs

    CO₂ Elimination
    • • Remove large amounts of CO₂ (approximately 200 mL / minute or ~18 L/hour).
    • • Eliminate significant quantities of water in the form of vapor every day.

    2. Liver (Largest Gland)

    Bile Secretions
    • • Secretes bile-containing substances: bilirubin, biliverdin, cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins, and drugs.
    • • Most pass out along with digestive wastes in feces.

    3. Skin & Salivary Glands

    Sweat & Sebum
    • • Sweat Glands: Produce sweat containing NaCl, small amounts of urea, lactic acid. Primary role: cooling effect.
    • • Sebaceous Glands: Eliminate sterols, hydrocarbons, and waxes through sebum (protective oily covering).
    • • Saliva: Small amounts of nitrogenous wastes can be eliminated through saliva.

    11 16.8 Disorders of the Excretory System

    NCERT Page 213-214

    1. Uremia & Hemodialysis (Artificial Kidney)

    Critical Condition

    Uremia: Malfunctioning of kidneys leading to accumulation of high levels of urea in blood; highly harmful and may lead to kidney failure.

    Step-by-Step Procedure of Hemodialysis:
    1. Blood drained from a convenient artery is pumped into a dialysing unit (artificial kidney).
    2. An anticoagulant like HEPARIN is added to the drained blood.
    3. The dialysing unit contains a coiled cellophane tube surrounded by dialysing fluid having same composition as plasma except NO nitrogenous wastes.
    4. Porous cellophane membrane allows nitrogenous wastes to freely diffuse down concentration gradient into dialysing fluid, clearing the blood.
    5. Cleared blood is pumped back into patient's body through a VEIN after adding ANTI-HEPARIN.

    2. Kidney Transplantation

    • Ultimate method in correction of acute renal failure (kidney failure).

    • A functioning kidney from a donor, preferably a close relative, is transplanted to minimize chances of immune rejection by host system.

    3. Calculi & Inflammatory Disorders

    • Renal Calculi: Stone or insoluble mass of crystallised salts (oxalates, etc.) formed within the kidney.
    • Glomerulonephritis: Inflammation of glomeruli of kidney.
    Disorder Pathology / Key Cause Clinical Features & Management
    Uremia Kidney failure leading to urea buildup in blood Toxic urea accumulation; managed via Hemodialysis or transplant
    Renal Calculi Precipitation of insoluble salt crystals (calcium oxalates) Severe pain (renal colic); obstructs urine flow in kidney/ureter
    Glomerulonephritis Immune-mediated inflammation of glomeruli Hematuria, proteinuria, hypertension, reduced GFR
    Diabetes Mellitus Insulin deficiency / resistance leading to hyperglycemia Glycosuria and Ketonuria detected in urine examination

    📝 COMPLETE NCERT TEXTBOOK EXERCISE SOLUTIONS

    All 12 Textbook Questions Solved with Accurate NCERT Academic Language

    Answers Verified

    Q1. Define Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR).

    Answer: Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) is defined as the total amount of filtrate formed by the glomeruli of both kidneys per minute. In a healthy adult human individual, the normal GFR is approximately 125 mL/minute, which amounts to roughly 180 Litres per day.

    Q2. Explain the autoregulatory mechanism of GFR.

    Answer: Kidneys possess a built-in autoregulatory feedback mechanism to regulate GFR, primarily carried out by the Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA):

    • JGA is a specialized cellular modification formed at the point of contact between the Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) and the afferent arteriole.

    • A fall in GFR or glomerular blood pressure activates the Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells to release the enzyme Renin.

    • Renin stimulates conversion of angiotensinogen to Angiotensin II, which constricts arterioles and increases glomerular blood flow, restoring GFR back to normal.

    Q3. Indicate whether the following statements are true or false:

    (a) Micturition is carried out by a reflex. ➔ TRUE (Micturition reflex via CNS motor signals).

    (b) ADH helps in water elimination, making the urine hypotonic. ➔ FALSE (ADH promotes water reabsorption, making urine hypertonic/concentrated).

    (c) Protein-free fluid is filtered from blood plasma into the Bowman's capsule. ➔ TRUE (Proteins are retained in plasma during ultrafiltration).

    (d) Henle's loop plays an important role in concentrating the urine. ➔ TRUE (Creates hyperosmolar medullary gradient).

    (e) Glucose is actively reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule. ➔ TRUE (Reabsorbed actively against concentration gradient).

    Q4. Give a brief account of the counter current mechanism.

    Answer: The counter-current mechanism operates between the two limbs of the Loop of Henle and the two limbs of Vasa Recta to produce concentrated urine:
    1. Flow of filtrate in Henle's loop limbs and flow of blood in Vasa recta limbs run in opposite directions (counter-current).
    2. NaCl is transported out by ascending limb of Henle's loop and enters descending limb of Vasa Recta, and is returned to medullary interstitium by ascending Vasa Recta.
    3. Small amounts of urea enter thin ascending limb and are returned to interstitium by collecting duct.
    4. This maintains an increasing osmolarity gradient from 300 mOsmol L⁻¹ in cortex to 1200 mOsmol L⁻¹ in inner medulla, enabling easy osmotic extraction of water from collecting duct to concentrate urine 4x.

    Q5. Describe the role of liver, lungs and skin in excretion.

    Answer:

    • Lungs: Excrete approximately 200 mL of CO₂ per minute along with significant quantities of water vapor during respiration.

    • Liver: Secretes bile containing bilirubin, biliverdin, cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins, and drugs, which pass out with digestive wastes.

    • Skin: Sweat glands eliminate sweat (containing NaCl, small amounts of urea, lactic acid). Sebaceous glands eliminate sterols, hydrocarbons, and waxes via sebum.

    Q6. Explain micturition.

    Answer: Micturition is the voluntary/involuntary process of release of urine from the urinary bladder through the urethra. As urine accumulates, stretch receptors on bladder walls send sensory signals to the CNS. The CNS responds with motor signals triggering simultaneous contraction of smooth detrusor muscles of the bladder and relaxation of the urethral sphincter, causing expulsion of urine via the micturition reflex.

    Q8. What is meant by the term osmoregulation?

    Answer: Osmoregulation is the physiological process by which an organism maintains the optimal balance of water content, total solute concentration, and osmotic pressure in its body fluids to ensure homeostatic cellular function.

    Q9. Terrestrial animals are generally either ureotelic or uricotelic, not ammonotelic. Why?

    Answer: Ammonia is highly toxic and requires a very large volume of water for its elimination (it is excreted by diffusion across surfaces in aquatic environments). Terrestrial habitats face water scarcity. Therefore, terrestrial animals convert toxic ammonia into less toxic urea (ureotelism) or non-toxic insoluble uric acid (uricotelism) to conserve body water.

    Q10. What is the significance of juxta glomerular apparatus (JGA) in kidney function?

    Answer: JGA acts as a crucial autoregulatory sensor in the kidney. When blood pressure or GFR drops, JGA secretes Renin, initiating the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS). Angiotensin II constricts blood vessels and Aldosterone increases Na⁺/water reabsorption, thereby restoring normal blood pressure and GFR.

    Q11. Name the following:

    (a) A chordate animal having flame cells as excretory structures: ➔ Amphioxus (Cephalochordata)
